The tool is bin/tie-min; the counting is bin/tie-scan; the tests are tests/test_tie_min.py and tests/test_tie_scan.py. 1. The case, in plain terms (agent's reading)

What a transcription pipeline does with durations. A machine that turns a recording or a MIDI file into notation ends with a list of lengths: this note lasted 1.31 s, the next onset came 0.63 s later. It rounds each length to a grid, then has to write every rounded length as noteheads. A length that is not one notehead (theorem 1: a single run of 1s in binary, such as 3/4 or 7/16) has to be tied. So the step "write this length" is exactly the question T(d) answers: how many noteheads? His statement names the pipeline: "the recording to score pipeline", "generate score for players, render score from players", "pitch to MIDI tools like Ableton's" (essays/brown/portal/statement.txt, essays/harvard/portal/statement.txt, and the other programs' copies).

What the notation programs do. From novelty.md (source read 2026-11-27): Abjad cuts the binary expansion at its 0s, one notehead per block, so it writes 21/16 as 16 + 4 + 1 sixteenths (c1~ c4~ c16, three noteheads); music21 "will break off the largest type that fits within this type and recurse". Neither minimises noteheads. I did not read LilyPond's own splitting code, MuseScore's, or Dorico's, so "notation programs tie by binary blocks or greedily" is shown here for Abjad and music21 only.

What the shortest tie saves. bin/tie-min 21/16 prints the binary tie (3 noteheads) and a shortest tie (2): c1~ c4~ c16 against c2...~ c4.. The saving is never more than about half (theorem B: the binary tie has at most 2T - 1 noteheads). Whether a reader prefers three plain noteheads to two with dots, three of them on one head, is a different question (section 5); the tool counts, it does not judge.

Run it:

bin/tie-min 21/16 # binary tie 3, shortest 2 bin/tie-min --unit 1/32 21 # the same duration as 21 thirty-seconds bin/tie-min --max-dots 2 341/256 # unbounded 3 noteheads, at most 2 dots 4 bin/tie-min --all 5/4 # both shortest ties: c1~ c4 and c2.~ c2

The --max-dots N search is the bounded one, outside the theorems (they allow any number of dots): it is a breadth-first search over sums of runs of at most N+1 bits, not the profile programme. The unbounded answers come from bin/duration-ties' Theorem P code by import.

2. What the counts say (the honest result first)

On Zach's own LilyPond, hand-written or not marked as generated, the result never bites: 77 tie chains in 12 files, and in none does a shortest tie beat the binary tie (b > T: 0), and in none do two dots cost a notehead (T2 > T: 0). Every one of the 67 chains that a shorter tie could replace is a chain whose total is a single notehead (for example 4~4 = 1/2, 1~1 = 2, 4~2. = 1), a barline or beat tie that the regex cannot tell from a duration-forced one.

It bites in machine-built scores: the pipeline output (the film-b water scores from bin/plunge, and the three Siddhartha 2023 scores whose headers say "WRITTEN BY build_show.py / build.py" and "Agent-derived draft ... from machine passes over the church board recording"): 378 chains in 5 files, 4 where the shortest tie beats the binary tie, 16 where two dots cost a notehead. So the practical case, on this data, is the recording-to-score pipeline and nothing hand-written yet, and it is a small case: the 4 chains are 21/16, 21/32 (twice) and 43/32. The much larger effect in the same files is not the shortest tie at all but the plain merge: 179 of the 378 machine-built chains sum to one notehead, which a pipeline that sums before it splits would write as one note (243 of the 378 have a shorter tie than the one written).

What the regular expressions read and miss (bin/tie-scan's docstring has the full list; these move the counts):

  • It reads text, not LilyPond: no \include is followed, no variable or Scheme expression is evaluated, \repeat unfold bodies count once.
  • A chain is the same pitch name (octave marks dropped, since \relative drops them) joined by ~, in source order. In << ... \\ ... >> the voices are read one after the other, so a chain is lost where the other voice's notes fall between two tied notes.
  • A bare duration (1~ 2) takes the last pitch; one that follows a rest, or starts a file, ties to nothing, so Eris-style { 1~ 2 r } after a rest is missed.
  • ~ inside a chord, \repeatTie, \laissezVibrer, \afterGrace, and notes written as q are not read.
  • Pitch-like words in lyrics or markup outside strings may be read as notes; each matters only next to a ~.
  • The metre tracker loses its place in \repeat volta with \alternative, \partial inside voices and \cadenza, so the bar cuts are right only in the 4 verified files.
  • 0 tuplet chains were found, which is a count, not a proof there are none: \tuplet is read only in the forms \tuplet n/m {, \times n/m {, \scaleDurations n/m {.
  • "Machine-built" is decided by path (film-b, check-2023/pitches) or a header ("WRITTEN BY", "Agent-derived"); a hand-written file that looks machine-built, or the reverse, shifts the split. The Eris charts (portfolio/eris/) and the abc/ cues are counted as not generated; the abc/1m2.ily header calls itself "Pure musical data"; whether the abc/ cues are transcriptions of recordings I did not check.

2b. A quantised performance (bin/tie-scan tsv)

What exists: no MIDI is input to his pipeline in the repo (the one .mid, portfolio/film-b/plunge-stitch-v4-water-stitch.mid, is written by bin/plunge/transcribe.py from the same notes the TSV lists, so it adds nothing). What does exist is the pipeline's own timing tables, from his recordings: the audio-to-MIDI transcription of the film-b water clips, and the sung held tones of Total Abstraction 2023-10-21. Nothing here is synthesised; the command reads those TSVs.

Method: onset and offset in seconds, converted to a fraction of a whole note at quarter = 60 (a whole note is 4 s, the repo's own grid note in plunge-stitch-v4-water-score.ly: "an eighth at quarter = 60"), quantised to 1/32 and to 1/48 of a whole note. An inter-onset interval is next onset minus this onset within one part (onsets on one grid point merged); a note length is offset minus onset. "Need a tie" = more than one block (b >= 2). On the 1/48 grid most values land on a triplet and are not dyadic (corollary 1a: no tie reaches them); they are counted apart and not tied here, so the 1/48 rows below are the dyadic remainder only.

4. What would make this bite (a list; the work, not a sentence for the paper)

  1. A dots bound. The theorems are unbounded; every real tool stops at two or three dots. The bounded version is tie-min --max-dots N, a search with no proof behind it. A theorem that is sharp under "at most N dots" (the binary tie splits each block of length L into ceil(L/(N+1)) noteheads, so the bounded T is between that and the unbounded T) would be the result a tool author needs. 16 of 455 chains in 2a are where it matters.
  2. A metre. Most ties are forced by a bar line or a beat (proof-notes.md section 6). The scanner's bar-cut pass is the beginning: 141 of 455 chains cross a computed bar line. A beat model (4/4 does not tie across beat 3) would turn the "upper bound" count into a measured one.
  3. A pipeline that sums before it splits. The larger effect in the machine-built scores is the plain merge (179 of 378 chains are one notehead written as two or more; 243 have a shorter tie). A pipeline stage that takes a rounded duration and calls tie-min once is the whole of this case, and costs little: shortest_runs took about 70 microseconds per duration under 2^20 (2,000 random odd durations, timed once on this machine).
  4. A readability study. Does a reader prefer c1~ c4~ c16 to c2...~ c4.? Nothing in the repo or the sources read says; question 1 of proof-notes.md section 8 is the same question to Zach. Without it "shortest" is a count, not a quality.
  5. More than Abjad and music21. The claim that notation programs tie by binary blocks or greedily is shown for those two. LilyPond's own splitting (barlines), MuseScore's importer (a forum post in novelty.md says it ties greedily; search-confirmed, not read), and Dorico and Sibelius (closed) were not read.
  6. His own pipeline's numbers. The grid choices (1/32, 1/48, quarter = 60) are mine; the real quantisation his pipeline would use is a decision of his.
