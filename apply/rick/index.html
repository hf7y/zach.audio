The loop, and what keeps people in it

It's the feeling that being a part of it is so valuable that you'll labor to have the thing continue to exist so that you can be a part of it.
Abe put so much of his uncompensated labor in over ten years that he handed off a thing that's core profitable. Vaporwave makes more money than it costs despite zero founder labor.
How can art bring people together to make more art that brings people together to make art?
What's to be wary of is the invisible labor.

The thesis

What music persuades its recipients to become senders? The object of study is the anthem which is communication of an identity through performance. The recipients of that identity are persuaded to perform a social function through the motivation of identification. The performance of that social function demands further musical use. I have rounded that loop many times already, though it has never outlived me. I have two examples of that loop returning with an artifact (performance or recording), but it gets blocked on starting the next cycle. I'm going to land in a place. My work is going to be written for professional musicians (of whatever ensemble the school has to work with). Then those musicians with me are going to play for an audience not of listeners, but amateur performers, who will identify with the work and also be able to adapt it. The loop closes on itself when I can exit. The works I'm creating are anthems, or charters, essentially founding documents which lay out the development of the self-perpetuating performance community. The performance of the anthem models the community's particular guardrails around adaptation. The ensemble closed the loop without me and we can study the output: how has it been adapted? why? The final portfolion includes, anthems I've written, along with works composed in the course of looping up until the moment of the defense.
