The proposal

I'm investigating how to use a team of professionals to kickstart a community of musicians that sustains itself.
The model is a blend of devised work and notated instruction. The workshop period begins with a score played by a professional ensemble.
The goal is to develop a system that I can cleanly hand off to community members who have become stakeholders, trained on the approach, and with a motivation model that sustains the loop.

The thesis

professionals teach amateurs the anthem, then amateurs meet and play the anthem and draft the future music with me. then I exit the loop when notation is produces without my hand once

The model

Eris: the Krewe of Eris, a Mardi Gras parade band in New Orleans in which Zach plays tuba.

The model is the anthem, prime reference is Anthem for Eris. Anthem embodies the ethos of the community just like Ken's person-specific material does. In this case the community-specific writing is for the decision making model represented by varying degrees of agency (adaptation) granted to the community members.
They take on the identity of being "in" the thing. But being "in" Eris is slippery. It's unsanctioned, unpermitted, anyone can show up and play along. There are no floats. The attendees dress to the theme to demonstrate their inside knowledge, so the second line (the walkers behind the band) have the identity too (they know the bit, got an "invitation" and exulisve bit of hand made ephemera showing the theme)
The old guard argues amongst itself

The first two semesters

The first semester tests the recording to score pipeline with me in the loop. I hand build the scores while aligning the mechanism to reproduce the work. The next semester I hand off the scoring and mechanism, training others to run it.

Works

Love Me (Long)

Score Listen Watch

Performers: Nicolee Kuester, Daniel Lippel, Jacqueline Kerrod

This piece was inspired by my grandmother who loves Elvis. I have fond memories sitting with her at the piano bench singing and playing songs when I was very young. With "Love Me (Long)," I experimented with creating a gradient of dissonance to consonance. I was surprised to discover the effect this had on me as a listener yearning for the hidden beauty to emerge.

I quote Elvis in Love Me (Long) and give away the bit at the end. It's the opposite of an inside joke.
"Love Me (Long)" melds the timbres of plucked strings and horn together using pulsing rhythm developed in "Soke" percussion.
Love Me has the best harmonic ideas.
The form of Love Me is the most sophisticated.

Astrocasper

Score Listen

Performers: Mivos Quartet, Matthew Gold

I wrote Astrocasper for the Walden summer music festival in New Hampshire. "Casper" refers to the harmonies of Ravel's Gaspard de la nuit. First, I lull the audience into a noisy romantic daydream. Then I break the spell with theater and irreverent quotations of Radiohead and J-Dilla.

In Astrocasper, I instruct the players to bow silently, misdirection for the listener. The score is like the Dungeon Master's screen; some arcane secrecy is useful for effect.
Astrocasper demonstrates the positioning of the bow and varying pressure in the left hand creating spectra of varying harmonicity.
Astrocasper has the score literally disappear until it's just lines or blank notes.

Siddhartha / Total Abstraction

Watch: Total Abstraction, 2023 revision, 2023 performance (9:50) Score: Act 2, 2023 performance (PDF) Watch: Total Abstraction, 2023 revision, uplight edit, 480p preview (10:08)

I set Siddhartha, based on Herman Hesses' orientalist original, in a Christian church, something I know nothing about; it's a reverse orientalism, occidentalism?, where I make similar foreignisms as Hesse does but about a different culture, a hegemonic one.
In my opera Siddhartha, I worked with an ensemble of dancers and singers, professionals and amateurs, to develop the vocal and movement material for the second act.
In Siddhartha, I represented the philosophies of the characters journey through the compositional discipline associated with each: "Total Abstration" for example.

67

Score

Performers: Nouveau Classical Project (2019), Amorisma Trio (2020)

This piece was my first application of the devised method of directing which I initially studied in the context of theater. I worked with instrumental musicians in rehearsal to craft an ethereal performance inspired by French astronomer Camille Flammarion's hot-air balloon flight in 1867. The astronomer describes hearing a concert of instrumental music emanating from a region of dense cloud, possibly due to atmospheric diffraction of a performance taking place on the ground.

My piece '67 which I did both with Nouveaux Classical and at UNT in Denton, I used a vocoder patch with a coached ensemble.

Soke

Score Listen

Performers: Bent Duo

"Soke" was my first composition to premier at Tulane. I followed a through-composed logic inspired by Toshio Hosokowa. I wrote measure to measure, letting each musical idea inspire the next. I aimed to construct a unified 'meta-instrument' between piano and percussion. Tremolos and small subdivisions are passed between instruments as they cooperate to express each gesture.

Chiasmus, transcribed for the Krewe of Eris

Score: B♭ clarinet part, 2 pp. (PDF) Listen: Krewe of Eris, 2015 recording (before his 2023 transcription) (9:39)

In "Chiasmus" transcribed for Eris, I wrote out the secret Latin choral part, but I whited out the noteheads and the lyrics. The material is still spaced to fit, but learning the music is left as an exercise for the player. You need to go to rehearsal and learn it socially.
