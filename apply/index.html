The thesis

What music persuades its recipients to become senders? The composer (the musical "user") adapts absolute (Dahlhaus) stimulation and programmed associations to produce a message. That message persuades recipients to effect changes that reinforce the social function that produces similar messages. The loop is closed by identification as members of the group reenact the ritual while performing the shared identity. The loop develops through adaptation as the performance evolves, shifting the identity it communicates which changes the effect on recipients. What music do individuals in their freedom choose to reproduce, and why?

Works

Love Me (Long)

Score

Nicolee Kuester, Daniel Lippel, Jacqueline Kerrod

This piece was inspired by my grandmother who loves Elvis. I have fond memories sitting with her at the piano bench singing and playing songs when I was very young. With "Love Me (Long)," I experimented with creating a gradient of dissonance to consonance. I was surprised to discover the effect this had on me as a listener yearning for the hidden beauty to emerge.

Love Me has the best harmonic ideas.
The form of Love Me is the most sophisticated.

Astrocasper

Score

Mivos Quartet, Matthew Gold

I wrote Astrocasper for the Walden summer music festival in New Hampshire. "Casper" refers to the harmonies of Ravel's Gaspard de la nuit. First, I lull the audience into a noisy romantic daydream. Then I break the spell with theater and irreverent quotations of Radiohead and J-Dilla.

Astrocasper has the score literally disappear until it's just lines or blank notes.

Ken Ueno

Writing for the person; composition as a relationship

  • Love Me (Long)
Write for the person, not the instrument.
With Prof. Ueno, I recognized that composers actively building relationships with performers *was* composition—just as much as inking the page.
any attempt to drill down what that thing is was unwelcome, presumably because it disrupted some unity and stability.
I'm not even sure manipulation and care are proper antonyms. Care often motivates manipulation.

The loop, and what keeps people in it

  • Siddhartha / Total Abstraction
It's the feeling that being a part of it is so valuable that you'll labor to have the thing continue to exist so that you can be a part of it.
Abe put so much of his uncompensated labor in over ten years that he handed off a thing that's core profitable. Vaporwave makes more money than it costs despite zero founder labor.
How can art bring people together to make more art that brings people together to make art?
What's to be wary of is the invisible labor.
