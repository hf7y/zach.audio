Composing is my practice of spiritual and philosophical contemplation. This inquiry is only complete when it comes to life in my communities. From New Orleans, I understand that the work should be deeply embedded in community such that the makers and the patrons are all invested collectively in the expression. I do enjoy the founder position and operations engineering, bootstrapping clubs. I am not a cheerleader; I can't fire people up. I do, however, notice where the fire is already burning.

I have the insight now of having worked in a church, providing music literally as a "service". I've seen how much music serves. I've seen how rituals facilitate a connectedness to the cosmos, to each other, to the self. Academically, I want to build on theories to make that potency legible. I want to model for my peers the sense of responsibility I feel.

I'm looking for a community of theorist, support for a longitudinal project, and generally a different environment to refresh my perspectives. I need to see what's possible outside of here. I need to figure out what elements of my theory are place specific, and which ones are transposable. If there are lessons here that can be brought elsewhere, it would make the world a better place to do so. Similarly, there may be insights I find in other communities that can pay of for my communities here. My hypothesis, the hope, is that one we kick-start the engine, it can hum without me. Leaving here is essential for putting my hypothesis to the test. I will continue to come back for ritual loops like Carnival.

I'm investigating how to use a team of professionals to kickstart a community of musicians that sustains itself. It's the relationship between social function and persuasion that's interesting. The insight here is that the senders and receivers are actually the same population or at least of the same population. That's Steven Brown. He mentions how in direct transmission of music, often it's the same people on both sides. In contrast, with the industrial model, senders are specialists and receivers are consumers. So the insight here is about persuasion. What music persuades its recipients to become senders? Music can be freely reproduced. What music do individuals in their freedom choose to reproduce, and why?

The music generates a thing that is an identity. People identify with that. Then their identification demands more of the thing they use to identify that sharpens over time. The audience develops an aesthetic that certain artistic developments resonate more with certain crowds. That gets fed back into the artists who make the material to give it back to the people. That's the closed loop. I'm interested in things like loops that aren't purely just aesthetic, ritual loops, loops that talk about the passage of time, or that are productive in ways so that things ride on the loop. For example, communities that do charity activity, even though the core loop is recreational, that's the social aid and pleasure club. The pleasure makes the loop. The social aid rides the pleasure. It's the feeling that being a part of it is so valuable that you'll labor to have the thing continue to exist so that you can be a part of it. The loop is closed by identification as members of the group reenact the ritual while performing the shared identity. The loop develops through adaptation as the performance evolves, shifting the identity it communicates which changes the effect on recipients. The premise is that by constructing this loop, the musical use will reflect the social function desired by the recipients. Can this model be transposed from the New Orleans context with music used as the programming language, the charter, the anthem, to manifest closed loops. It's an open question whether I can coin a new identity. Right now, my assumption is to align with existing identities.

Our open-membership group plays a balance of mid-century folk rock revival and millennial alternative rock. I'm the master and I solved the modulations, but the group of varying abilities achieved it. In my opera Siddhartha, I worked with an ensemble of dancers and singers, professionals and amateurs, to develop the vocal and movement material for the second act. The professionals, paid by me, created the opportunity for the amateurs to have the elevated experience. Siddhartha was a good premise, I just didn't close the loop (church sold the building before I could spend my Jazz and Heritage Foundation Grant). In Film B, I use computation in the notation domain: generate score for players, render score from players. These are loops.

I have rounded that loop many times already, though it has never outlived me. Krewe of Vaporwave, Krewe of Eris, the basic Mardi Gras loop is tried and true. What's to be wary of is the invisible labor. Abe put so much of his uncompensated labor in over ten years that he handed off a thing that's core profitable. Vaporwave makes more money than it costs despite zero founder labor. That's the kick starting trick. If you have to keep kicking, that's a bicycle not a motorcycle. I think a lot about organizations like this, how they often make or break when the founders seek to retire. Can they give up power with the confidence that the vision is protected? The loop that closes is what matters most. As the loop repeats, it can make aesthetic progress etc. That's the difference between an engine and a bomb. The bomb uses all its fuel at once and is more about finality than continuity.

The object of study is the anthem which is communication of an identity through performance. The recipients of that identity are persuaded to perform a social function through the motivation of identification. The performance of that social function demands further musical use. The model is the anthem, prime reference is Anthem for Eris. Anthem embodies the ethos of the community just like Ken's person-specific material does. In this case the community-specific writing is for the decision making model represented by varying degrees of agency (adaptation) granted to the community members. They take on the identity of being "in" the thing. But being "in" Eris is slippery. It's unsanctioned, unpermitted, anyone can show up and play along. There are no floats. The Anthem for Eris notably starts where performers may choose their voice number, playing from an <0126> tetrachord. No rules effectively determine who plays what. Sections may confer and count off numbers, but if someone were to switch it would be hardly detectable. In fact the <0126> set is dissonant enough that any "wrong note" would be difficult to hear anyway. Thus the Anthem for Eris embodies the political principles that organize the group itself. The performance of the anthem models the community's particular guardrails around adaptation. This is variable: concensus, elective monarchy, anarchy are all plausible principles that may organize the group. The anthem should model this principle in the course of performing it. The music has within it the logic of how its use can be adapted. Adaptation must be possible but stability is necessary as well, likely so that identification continues to motivate as well. The stable part is simply the closure of the loop: musical use has persuaded recipients to produce the social conditions that beget more music. professionals teach amateurs the anthem, then amateurs meet and play the anthem and draft the future music with me. then I exit the loop when notation is produces without my hand once

Care is determined by what closes the loop. Identification is a strong force of motivation because exclusion is incredibly painful. So when I'm in a position of power, I'm always wary of how groups form cliques, how insiders posses advantages, and how incumbents may suppress entrants even when that harms the group as a whole. I think groups should always be cycling their membership in a dynamic way, not in a capitalist growth way, but to affirm the freedom to both join and to depart. For departure, knowing one can leave and come back is especially important. My intuition is that identification is enhanced by reciprocity, to be fair. I'm not even sure manipulation and care are proper antonyms. Care often motivates manipulation. And people often seek to manipulate themselves when they perceive it's possible to change for the better. For example sobriety groups manipulate each other to stay on the wagon.

The model is a blend of devised work and notated instruction. The workshop period begins with a score played by a professional ensemble. I go to a university. I begin working with an ensemble in social context. This is the bootstrap ensemble, professionals or otherwise masters. My work is going to be written for professional musicians (of whatever ensemble the school has to work with). Then those musicians with me are going to play for an audience not of listeners, but amateur performers, who will identify with the work and also be able to adapt it. Recipients are community members, guests invited to the open performance. From first principles, we know that recipients must be technically capable of becoming senders, of producing the message. They can be trained to do so. Recipients walk out being able to send the message. Notation is the programming. Master players perform the notation. The next week, masters play a "compressed" version of the previous week's material. The question is what governs that feedback so the occasion of play can produce knowledge about itself. Is there a way to capture facts about pleasure as it pertains to a community in a way that can inform future manifestations of a composition? How can we use repeated iteration to learn about what performs a social function better or worse such that the community is motivated to reproduce itself. Music is a ritual of obeying instruction. The work requires the practice because notation is the symbology of the input, it's the incantation of control. Does this resulting ritual loop stabilize with varying periodicity?

I have experimented with some initial pitch to MIDI tools like Ableton's combined with per-instrument microphones or near it. But oral tradition, non-spoken, performance practice layer certainly matters. In the parade, my abstention is legible. I can refuse to join a chant, crucially. With the pressing of the music into a record, compression reduces the dynamic range, making my abstention inaudible, my silence is silenced. So compression and filtering become parameters in the signal chain. In fact, the proposal here is almost to model Brown's flow chart as an actual signal flow, in Pure Data, for example, with notation being mediated by a Lilypond layer and a human performance layer. The first semester tests the recording to score pipeline with me in the loop. I hand build the scores while aligning the mechanism to reproduce the work. The next semester I hand off the scoring and mechanism, training others to run it.

Lessons can be taken from sacred music, liturgy, such as the liturgical calendar, the mass parts proper and ordinary. This genealogy possesses wisdom about interlocking ritual rhythms. Other art is found in the format clock of music radio. It describes a formula for attention that is also cyclical, stable but not static. Ordinary is present in every iteration. Meanwhile, the proper loops at a period closer to an annum. This is the question of form. Strictly ordinary is what's requires in every instance, and proper is anything with a period greater than that.

The goal is to develop a system that I can cleanly hand off to community members who have become stakeholders, trained on the approach, and with a motivation model that sustains the loop. The loop closes on itself when I can exit. The idea is that I can exit the loop both financially and in labor terms. The musical use must perform the identity while allowing for some adaptation based on measured effects on recipients, possibly self-reported, possibly as the result of recipients being senders themselves. Some recipients have become senders diversely motivated by identification and adaptation; adaptation motivated senders modify the communication. The goal of year five is that the communication has been modified without me.

My current idea is to write anthems and charter many nacent communities, bootstrapping to various intensities based on what initial work accomplishes. Writing an EP to start a band is an example: it would be a large open ensemble band whose rehearsals themselves serve a social function, with a culminating live performance that gets people onto a mailing list. Choirs are an instance of the model. I could create a single overarching legal structure but the legal structure doesn't need to come first. The final portfolio includes anthems I've written, along with works composed in the course of looping up until the moment of the defense. Lessons learned from earlier seasons will inform the ultimate season. A large multimedia work built from fragments of earlier iterations and built on lessons from them, built on relationships with players, venues, a broader community, and a written commentary on successes and failures with theory, all go before the committee
