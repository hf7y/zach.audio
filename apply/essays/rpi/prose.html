Composing is my practice of spiritual and philosophical contemplation. This inquiry is only complete when it comes to life in my communities. I received a B.A. in Music from UC Berkeley where I studied digital signal processing and composition. With Prof. Ueno, I recognized that composers actively building relationships with performers was composition—just as much as inking the page. In my score for "By the time I see you in the morning", I worked with a mixed group of classical and jazz string players to produce an extended timbral palette, combining notation and verbal instruction to suit the players. I was doing public media art as part of school. Rick Snow put the grad students in touch with the krewe and we collaborated during Mardi Gras (Oedipus Max). It required a social hero like Rick Snow to see the connection and plug us in.

In the Krewe of Eris, literacy is questioned, sometimes lampooned. I play the tuba. One of the best players in town Todd Burdock leads our section. He doesn't read; he plays by ear. He turned to me, not in submission but in confidence, that I could keep the memory. And for a founding-era member like Todd, it was hard to feel confident enough to stop shouldering the burden. The technology of notation proves its usefulness, it serves community. I think a lot about organizations like this, how they often make or break when the founders seek to retire. Can they give up power with the confidence that the vision is protected?

One year I decided to bring in my friend Chiara who played the clarinet in highschool. As a labor of love, I transcribed the woodwind parts. I made a book for just for her, something private and intimate. When the other wind players got word of the book, they started coming to me and asking for copies: something in Eb perhaps. It served the community by opening it up to new amateurs bringing their love to the group. I created a new snapshot of the present moment. It too will be drifted away from, and that signifies the aliveness of the community. In "Chiasmus" transcribed for Eris, I wrote out the secret Latin choral part, but I whited out the noteheads and the lyrics. The material is still spaced to fit, but learning the music is left as an exercise for the player. You need to go to rehearsal and learn it socially.

I provide sheet music, and have also created notated arrangements to help with rehearsal direction. I used Lilypond's Scheme backend to produce infinitely varied permutations of musical motifs. My piece '67 which I did both with Nouveaux Classical and at UNT in Denton, I used a vocoder patch with a coached ensemble. That's computation in the audio domain. In Film B, I use computation in the notation domain: generate score for players, render score from players. Social domain is represented by Mardi Gras and teaching.

I have the insight now of having worked in a church, providing music literally as a "service". I've seen how much music serves. I've seen how rituals facilitate a connectedness to the cosmos, to each other, to the self. These experiences have also led to my belief that composers are therefore responsible for creating the social dynamics we want to see in the broader world. Academically, I want to build on theories to make that potency legible.

How can art bring people together to make more art that brings people together to make art? The idea is to create works that come alive and continue development out of my hands. It's an ethic. It creates a micro-polity. I'm really curious about gaming as a direction. I think that the play loop is a really good one to test.

I think an earned-revenue non-profit is a good starting place. I do enjoy the founder position and operations engineering, bootstrapping clubs. I am not a cheerleader; I can't fire people up. I do, however, notice where the fire is already burning. My bet is on what I know lasts: civic ritual, banding together, social aid and pleasure.

I'm looking for a community of theorist, support for a longitudinal project, and generally a different environment to refresh my perspectives. Improvisation, for example, is a means by which individuals exist as individual agents within a collective practice. At the least I would credit my players as composers alongside myself. I want to model for my peers the sense of responsibility I feel. From New Orleans, I understand that the work should be deeply embedded in community such that the makers and the patrons are all invested collectively in the expression. I need to figure out what elements of my theory are place specific, and which ones are transposable. My hypothesis, the hope, is that one we kick-start the engine, it can hum without me. Leaving here is essential for putting my hypothesis to the test. I will continue to come back for ritual loops like Carnival.

I'm investigating how to use a team of professionals to kickstart a community of musicians that sustains itself.

It's the relationship between social function and persuasion that's interesting. The insight here is that the senders and receivers are actually the same population or at least of the same population. That's Steven Brown. He mentions how in direct transmission of music, often it's the same people on both sides. In contrast, with the industrial model, senders are specialists and receivers are consumers. So the insight here is about persuasion. What music persuades its recipients to become senders? The object of study is the anthem which is communication of an identity through performance. The recipients of that identity are persuaded to perform a social function through the motivation of identification. The performance of that social function demands further musical use. professionals teach amateurs the anthem, then amateurs meet and play the anthem and draft the future music with me. then I exit the loop when notation is produces without my hand once

I have rounded that loop many times already, though it has never outlived me. Krewe of Vaporwave, Krewe of Eris, the basic Mardi Gras loop is tried and true. Abe put so much of his uncompensated labor in over ten years that he handed off a thing that's core profitable. Vaporwave makes more money than it costs despite zero founder labor. I'm interested in things like loops that aren't purely just aesthetic, ritual loops, loops that talk about the passage of time, or that are productive in ways so that things ride on the loop. For example, communities that do charity activity, even though the core loop is recreational, that's the social aid and pleasure club. The pleasure makes the loop. The social aid rides the pleasure. It's the feeling that being a part of it is so valuable that you'll labor to have the thing continue to exist so that you can be a part of it. The loop is closed by identification as members of the group reenact the ritual while performing the shared identity. The loop develops through adaptation as the performance evolves, shifting the identity it communicates which changes the effect on recipients. Can this model be transposed from the New Orleans context with music used as the programming language, the charter, the anthem, to manifest closed loops. It's an open question whether I can coin a new identity. Right now, my assumption is to align with existing identities.

Anthem embodies the ethos of the community just like Ken's person-specific material does. In this case the community-specific writing is for the decision making model represented by varying degrees of agency (adaptation) granted to the community members. They take on the identity of being "in" the thing. But being "in" Eris is slippery. It's unsanctioned, unpermitted, anyone can show up and play along. There are no floats. The performance of the anthem models the community's particular guardrails around adaptation. This is variable: concensus, elective monarchy, anarchy are all plausible principles that may organize the group. The anthem should model this principle in the course of performing it. The music has within it the logic of how its use can be adapted. Adaptation must be possible but stability is necessary as well, likely so that identification continues to motivate as well. The stable part is simply the closure of the loop: musical use has persuaded recipients to produce the social conditions that beget more music.

The model is a blend of devised work and notated instruction. The workshop period begins with a score played by a professional ensemble. I go to a university. I begin working with an ensemble in social context. This is the bootstrap ensemble, professionals or otherwise masters. My work is going to be written for professional musicians (of whatever ensemble the school has to work with). Then those musicians with me are going to play for an audience not of listeners, but amateur performers, who will identify with the work and also be able to adapt it. Recipients are community members, guests invited to the open performance. Notation is the programming. Master players perform the notation. The next week, masters play a "compressed" version of the previous week's material. Music is a ritual of obeying instruction. The work requires the practice because notation is the symbology of the input, it's the incantation of control. Does this resulting ritual loop stabilize with varying periodicity?

I have experimented with some initial pitch to MIDI tools like Ableton's combined with per-instrument microphones or near it. But oral tradition, non-spoken, performance practice layer certainly matters. In the parade, my abstention is legible. I can refuse to join a chant, crucially. With the pressing of the music into a record, compression reduces the dynamic range, making my abstention inaudible, my silence is silenced. So compression and filtering become parameters in the signal chain. In fact, the proposal here is almost to model Brown's flow chart as an actual signal flow, in Pure Data, for example, with notation being mediated by a Lilypond layer and a human performance layer. The first semester tests the recording to score pipeline with me in the loop. I hand build the scores while aligning the mechanism to reproduce the work. The next semester I hand off the scoring and mechanism, training others to run it.

Lessons can be taken from sacred music, liturgy, such as the liturgical calendar, the mass parts proper and ordinary. This genealogy possesses wisdom about interlocking ritual rhythms. Other art is found in the format clock of music radio. It describes a formula for attention that is also cyclical, stable but not static. Ordinary is present in every iteration. Meanwhile, the proper loops at a period closer to an annum. This is the question of form. Strictly ordinary is what's requires in every instance, and proper is anything with a period greater than that.

The goal is to develop a system that I can cleanly hand off to community members who have become stakeholders, trained on the approach, and with a motivation model that sustains the loop. The loop closes on itself when I can exit. The idea is that I can exit the loop both financially and in labor terms. Some recipients have become senders diversely motivated by identification and adaptation; adaptation motivated senders modify the communication. The goal of year five is that the communication has been modified without me.

Our open-membership group plays a balance of mid-century folk rock revival and millennial alternative rock. I'm the master and I solved the modulations, but the group of varying abilities achieved it. In my opera Siddhartha, I worked with an ensemble of dancers and singers, professionals and amateurs, to develop the vocal and movement material for the second act. The professionals, paid by me, created the opportunity for the amateurs to have the elevated experience. Siddhartha was a good premise, I just didn't close the loop (church sold the building before I could spend my Jazz and Heritage Foundation Grant).

My current idea is to write anthems and charter many nacent communities, bootstrapping to various intensities based on what initial work accomplishes. Writing an EP to start a band is an example: it would be a large open ensemble band whose rehearsals themselves serve a social function, with a culminating live performance that gets people onto a mailing list. Choirs are an instance of the model. I could create a single overarching legal structure but the legal structure doesn't need to come first. The final portfolio includes anthems I've written, along with works composed in the course of looping up until the moment of the defense.
