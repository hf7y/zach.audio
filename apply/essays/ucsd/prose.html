In the Krewe of Eris, literacy is questioned, sometimes lampooned. I play the tuba. One of the best players in town Todd Burdock leads our section. He doesn't read; he plays by ear. I hate for my literacy to imply an automatic priority over embodied knowledge. He turned to me, not in submission but in confidence, that I could keep the memory. It's obviously beautiful when culture is transmitted mouth to ear: intimate. But that's also fragile. And for a founding-era member like Todd, it was hard to feel confident enough to stop shouldering the burden. The technology of notation proves its usefulness, it serves community.

I think a lot about organizations like this, how they often make or break when the founders seek to retire. Can they give up power with the confidence that the vision is protected? How can art bring people together to make more art that brings people together to make art? What music persuades its recipients to become senders? professionals teach amateurs the anthem, then amateurs meet and play the anthem and draft the future music with me. then I exit the loop when notation is produces without my hand once

The object of study is the anthem which is communication of an identity through performance. The identity exists before the anthem. The performance of the anthem identifies the identity to the recipients. They take on the identity of being "in" the thing. But being "in" Eris is slippery. It's unsanctioned, unpermitted, anyone can show up and play along. I'm interested in things like loops that aren't purely just aesthetic, ritual loops, loops that talk about the passage of time, or that are productive in ways so that things ride on the loop. For example, communities that do charity activity, even though the core loop is recreational, that's the social aid and pleasure club. The pleasure makes the loop. The social aid rides the pleasure. It's an open question whether I can coin a new identity. Right now, my assumption is to align with existing identities.

I'm motivated by generosity rather than extraction in the face of a surplus. The performance of the anthem models the community's particular guardrails around adaptation. This is variable: concensus, elective monarchy, anarchy are all plausible principles that may organize the group. Identification is a strong force of motivation because exclusion is incredibly painful. So when I'm in a position of power, I'm always wary of how groups form cliques, how insiders posses advantages, and how incumbents may suppress entrants even when that harms the group as a whole. In the parade, my abstention is legible. I can refuse to join a chant, crucially.

I'm investigating how to use a team of professionals to kickstart a community of musicians that sustains itself. The model is a blend of devised work and notated instruction. The workshop period begins with a score played by a professional ensemble. The first semester tests the recording to score pipeline with me in the loop. I hand build the scores while aligning the mechanism to reproduce the work. The next semester I hand off the scoring and mechanism, training others to run it. The goal is to develop a system that I can cleanly hand off to community members who have become stakeholders, trained on the approach, and with a motivation model that sustains the loop. The goal of year five is that the communication has been modified without me. The final portfolio includes anthems I've written, along with works composed in the course of looping up until the moment of the defense.

I have rounded that loop many times already, though it has never outlived me. In my opera Siddhartha, I worked with an ensemble of dancers and singers, professionals and amateurs, to develop the vocal and movement material for the second act. The professionals, paid by me, created the opportunity for the amateurs to have the elevated experience. Siddhartha was a good premise, I just didn't close the loop (church sold the building before I could spend my Jazz and Heritage Foundation Grant). I am not a cheerleader; I can't fire people up. I do, however, notice where the fire is already burning.

One year I decided to bring in my friend Chiara who played the clarinet in highschool. As a labor of love, I transcribed the woodwind parts. It served the community by opening it up to new amateurs bringing their love to the group. The original compositions aren't played as written. Without institutional memory, the interventions into that text get lost and people default to the authorized version. I created a new snapshot of the present moment. It too will be drifted away from, and that signifies the aliveness of the community. In "Chiasmus" transcribed for Eris, I wrote out the secret Latin choral part, but I whited out the noteheads and the lyrics. The material is still spaced to fit, but learning the music is left as an exercise for the player. You need to go to rehearsal and learn it socially. How much or how little I notate, how much I specify, how closely I expect performers to adhere to my rules (defer to my authority as an author, really) is a proxy for the power structure that underlies the collaboration. Improvisation, for example, is a means by which individuals exist as individual agents within a collective practice.

Composing is my practice of spiritual and philosophical contemplation. This inquiry is only complete when it comes to life in my communities. With Prof. Ueno, I recognized that composers actively building relationships with performers was composition—just as much as inking the page. I have the insight now of having worked in a church, providing music literally as a "service". I've seen how much music serves. I've seen how rituals facilitate a connectedness to the cosmos, to each other, to the self. Academically, I want to build on theories to make that potency legible. I want to model for my peers the sense of responsibility I feel.

My piece '67 which I did both with Nouveaux Classical and at UNT in Denton, I used a vocoder patch with a coached ensemble. That's computation in the audio domain. In Film B, I use computation in the notation domain: generate score for players, render score from players. These are loops. "Love Me (Long)" melds the timbres of plucked strings and horn together using pulsing rhythm developed in "Soke" percussion. In Astrocasper, I instruct the players to bow silently, misdirection for the listener. The score is like the Dungeon Master's screen; some arcane secrecy is useful for effect. In Siddhartha, I represented the philosophies of the characters journey through the compositional discipline associated with each: "Total Abstration" for example.

UCSD is looking for something more like an opera that is devised over many seasons into a mature form that promises to continue evolving. The loop is the opera company that devises, stages, documents, etc. It's the cycle of production (one new work a season perhaps). Lessons learned from earlier seasons will inform the ultimate season. I'm looking for a community of theorist, support for a longitudinal project, and generally a different environment to refresh my perspectives. I need to figure out what elements of my theory are place specific, and which ones are transposable. Leaving here is essential for putting my hypothesis to the test. I will continue to come back for ritual loops like Carnival.
