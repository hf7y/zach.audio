c-01: opening: Todd's moment, the elder hands over the memory (259 words)

present.todd-01 (259 words)

In the Krewe of Eris, literacy is questioned, sometimes lampooned. I keep mentioning how what isn't written is the true arcana there. I play the tuba. One of the best players in town Todd Burdock leads our section. He doesn't read; he plays by ear. The Eris music is chaotic, irregular, atonal at times. He has roughly memorized it over 20 years of playing it, but it's clearly much more difficult to remember than a trad jazz bassline over changes. At first I way coy. I hate for my literacy to imply an automatic priority over embodied knowledge. I think often of an essay I read in Ana Sanchez Rojo's Music and Spirituality class. It was about the introduction of printed hymnals in the black church. It cited a poem, from the perspective of an elder singer. Once the culture bearer whom other singers asked to "line out" the hymns, the singer in the poem was now at the bottom of the hierarcy: asking others what the printed music said, what was authorized by an author. Yet, I noticed Todd our elder who like the sousaphone carried the knowledge of our section on his shoulders. He turned to me, not in submission but in confidence, that I could keep the memory. So it's a balance. It's obviously beautiful when culture is transmitted mouth to ear: intimate. But that's also fragile. And for a founding-era member like Todd, it was hard to feel confident enough to stop shouldering the burden. The technology of notation proves its usefulness, it serves community.

Got it. The insight here is that the senders and receivers are actually the same population or at least of the same population. That's Steven Brown.
